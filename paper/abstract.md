**Title:** When Does Gravity Matter? A Transport-Regime Criterion for Microbial and Particulate Delivery in ECLSS Water Loops Across Earth, Mars, and Lunar Gravity

Ground-based (1g) qualification testing is the default validation path for spacecraft Environmental Control and Life Support System (ECLSS) water hardware, but whether gravity-driven particle transport is negligible at Mars (0.38g) or lunar (0.17g) gravity — and therefore whether 1g testing is a valid surrogate — has not been quantified for the microbial and particulate populations documented in ISS water systems. We adapt an established transport-mechanism decomposition from colloid filtration theory (diffusion, interception, and gravitational sedimentation competing for wall delivery; Yao, Habibian & O'Melia, 1971) by making gravitational acceleration an explicit swept parameter, and evaluate it at real operating points for the ISS Water Processor Assembly, Urine Processor Assembly, and a lab-scale space-wastewater Membrane Aerated Biofilm Reactor, for particle classes spanning single planktonic cells (~1 µm) to biofilm-sloughed aggregates (up to 500 µm). We find that gravitational and diffusive wall-delivery fluxes for single non-motile cells are comparable to within an order of magnitude across the entire Earth-to-Moon gravity range (probability gravity dominates: 0.61 at Earth, 0.30 at Mars, 0.12 at Moon, propagated over sourced parameter uncertainty), so a 1g test is not a defensible surrogate for that population; gravity dominates delivery of aggregates larger than ~7 µm by two to three orders of magnitude at every gravity level tested, with a size-selective carry-through transition for 100–200 µm flocs across the lunar-to-Mars window. In a stagnant-line dormancy scenario, deposition rate (not final accumulated mass) is the gravity-sensitive quantity, differing ~1700-fold between Earth/Mars and lunar gravity via a buoyant-convection onset threshold. All results are modeling-only, validated against internally-consistent regression checks and published secondary data; an independent adversarial review found and this work corrected one implementation error and reframed the novelty claim. Physical validation, where warranted, is achievable with an inclined-clinostat fractional-gravity analog costing tens of dollars.
