# Atmosphere Revitalization: Literature Review and Candidate Research Gaps

## Sources

### CO2 Removal - Sorbent Bed Systems (CDRA)
- **"Optimization of the Carbon Dioxide Removal Assembly (CDRA-4EU) in Support of the International Space System and Advanced Exploration Systems"** — NASA NTRS, 2015, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20150016500. Describes optimization of the ISS 4-bed CDRA using alternating desiccant and CO2 adsorbent beds; focus on sorbent materials (RK-38, 13X zeolite) and bed configuration improvements.

- **"ISS Carbon Dioxide Removal Assembly (ISS CDRA) Concepts and Advancements"** — NASA NTRS, 2005, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/archive/nasa/casi.ntrs.nasa.gov/20050210002.pdf. Foundational overview of CDRA thermal/pressure swing adsorption cycle with two desiccant and two adsorbent beds; cyclic operation achieving continuous CO2 removal.

- **"CO2 Removal Onboard the International Space Station – 4-Bed Molecular Sieve System"** — NASA NTRS, 2019, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20190030375/downloads/20190030375.pdf (protected). Describes ISS 4BMS current performance; notes that desiccant bed 13X breakthrough masks true CO2 sorbent behavior, indicating optimization potential in bed design.

- **"Sorbent Bed Breakthrough Performance and Optimization"** — TTU ICES Repository, https://ttu-ir.tdl.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/5b7043f9-448f-4673-a486-74667ffbc554/content. Discusses competitive adsorption between H₂O and CO₂, exothermic adsorption heat management (ΔH = −145 kJ/mol), and proposed improvements including reduction of pellet size and use of structured sorbents.

### CO2 Reduction - Sabatier Process
- **"Compact, Lightweight Adsorber and Sabatier Reactor for Life Support Applications"** — NASA NTRS, 2012, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20120015003/downloads/20120015003.pdf (protected). Engineering development unit design for compact Sabatier methanation reactor; H₂ + CO₂ → CH₄ + H₂O with methane vented to space.

- **"Compact and Lightweight Sabatier Reactor for Carbon Dioxide Reduction"** — NASA NTRS, 2012, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20120016419/downloads/20120016419.pdf (protected). Technical specifications for flight-ready Sabatier reactor design for closed-loop ECLSS integration with OGA hydrogen source.

### CO2 Reduction - Bosch Process
- **"The Bosch Process"** — NASA NTRS, 2010, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20100033001/downloads/20100033001.pdf (protected). Exothermic reduction of CO₂ by H₂ in presence of Ni catalyst to form H₂O + solid carbon; enables 100% theoretical O₂ recovery vs. Sabatier's 50%.

- **"Performance Evaluation of Staged Bosch Process for CO2 Reduction"** — NASA NTRS, 2012, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/search.jsp?R=20120015344 (protected). Series-Bosch system for solid carbon production; developmental status and performance targets for long-duration missions.

- **"Bosch Reaction"** — Marspedia, https://marspedia.org/Bosch_reaction (paywalled). Describes catalytic nickel-based reactor for Bosch process; applicability to Mars in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) and spacecraft closed-loop operation.

### Oxygen Generation
- **"Advanced Oxygen Generation Assembly for Exploration Missions"** — NASA NTRS, 2019, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20190030425/downloads/20190030425.pdf (protected). OGA solid polymer electrolyte (SPE) design for water electrolysis; proton exchange membrane converts H₂O → H₂ + O₂ using ~120 W average power. ISS OGA has produced >16,000 lbm O₂ as of April 2019.

- **"Oxygen Generation Assembly Design for Exploration Missions"** — NASA NTRS, 2018, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20180006335. Advanced OGA development for deep-space; closed-loop architecture with Sabatier integration.

- **"Electrolysis in Reduced Gravitational Environments: Current Research Perspectives and Future Applications"** — npj Microgravity, 2022, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41526-022-00239-y (paywalled). Reviews electrolysis performance in microgravity; gas bubble behavior, electrode wetting, and mass transport differ from terrestrial conditions.

### Trace Contaminant Control

### Advanced CO2 Removal Concepts

## Candidate Research Gaps

### Gap 1: Sorbent Bed Cyclic Regeneration Optimization Under Competitive Water-CO2 Adsorption
**Prior Work:** ISS CDRA 4-bed system empirically optimized over ~25 years; recent NTRS papers identify that desiccant bed breakthrough masks true CO2 adsorbent performance. Exothermic adsorption heat (ΔH = −145 kJ/mol) and water-CO2 competitive adsorption known qualitatively.

**Why It Matters:** Water vapor significantly enhances CO2 breakthrough and sorbent bed cycling efficiency, but transient cyclic performance during temperature/pressure swing regeneration is incompletely characterized. For partial-gravity or longer-duration missions (Moon surface, Mars 2-3 year transit), understanding coupled water-CO2 dynamics could reduce regeneration energy by 10–20% and improve breakthrough prediction.

**Tractability:** Bench-scale packed-bed experiments with controlled moisture input, thermocouples, and breakthrough curve measurement. Requires no exotic hardware; pairing with transient adsorption-desorption models (Langmuir/BET kinetics) is achievable over 1–2 years for a modeling-focused contribution.

**Novelty/Tractability/Relevance Scores:**
- **Novelty: 6/10** — Competitive adsorption is well-understood at equilibrium; the gap is transient cyclic characterization and optimization. Not groundbreaking but addresses real ISS operational insight.
- **Tractability: 8/10** — Experiment is straightforward (lab-scale, no microgravity required); modeling bridges to mission design.
- **Relevance: 7/10** — Direct applicability to CDRA optimization and future sorbent-based systems; impact on mission power/mass budget is real but incremental (~5–10%).

**Score: 6–7/10** — Medium-priority, achievable contribution with clear engineering payoff.

### Gap 2: Gas-Liquid Phase Separation Mechanisms and Microgravity Design for Liquid Amine CO2 Removal
**Prior Work:** Liquid amine scrubbers (MEA, DGA, other amines) show 65% lower power/weight/volume than solid CDRA. Vortex phase separator (VPS) demonstrated feasibility in preliminary tests; capillary fluidic systems (Capisorb CVS) have flown on ISS but phase separation design for scaled systems remains under development. No flight-qualified, large-capacity liquid-amine CO2 removal system exists.

**Why It Matters:** For long-duration lunar base or Mars transit (18–30 months), mass and power constraints favor liquid amines over solid sorbents. However, phase separation of regenerated liquid from CO2-rich vapor in microgravity is a critical failure point. Current vortex separator designs are small-scale; scaling to crew-class habitats (100–1000 m³) without gravity-driven settling is unresolved. Partial-gravity (Moon ~0.16g, Mars ~0.38g) offers intermediate operating envelope not yet explored.

**Tractability:** Medium-to-high. Parabolic flight campaigns (0–10 second microgravity windows) can validate phase separator performance. Ground-based partial-gravity analog tests in centrifuge (e.g., NASA tractor-trailer human centrifuge) or rotating-chamber rig could simulate lunar/Mars conditions. CFD modeling of vortex/separation dynamics feasible; bench-scale component testing (separator, contactor, degasser) achievable in 2–3 years.

**Novelty/Tractability/Relevance Scores:**
- **Novelty: 8/10** — Microgravity engineering of liquid-amine regeneration is emerging; vortex separators, capillary separators, and electro-separation approaches all competing. High-novelty design space.
- **Tractability: 6/10** — Requires parabolic flight or centrifuge access; more complex than bench CDRA; realistic timescale 3–4 years for flight-ready component.
- **Relevance: 9/10** — Directly enables next-generation revitalization for NASA's Moon-to-Mars architecture; mass/power savings are 30–50%, mission-critical.

**Score: 7–8/10** — High-priority, addresses real technology gap for exploration missions.

### Gap 3: Humidity-CO2 Coupled Sorbent Materials and Single-Unit Revitalization
**Prior Work:** Current ISS CDRA uses separate desiccant beds (SG B 125, 13X zeolite) and CO2 adsorbent beds (RK-38). Literature mentions exploratory work on hybrid sorbents (e.g., amine-loaded zeolites) but no flight-qualified integrated system. Humidity control remains a separate subsystem (condensing heat exchanger + water recovery), adding mass/volume/power.

**Why It Matters:** For distributed or modular habitats (e.g., Lunar Gateway modules, multi-spacecraft Mars architecture), a single sorbent bed removing both CO2 and excess humidity would reduce ducting, thermal losses, and system complexity. Partial-gravity environments (Moon, Mars) may enable novel separation regimes not possible on Earth or ISS. Trade-off: selectivity loss, regeneration heat coupling, sorbent material cost/availability.

**Tractability:** Material screening via bench-scale thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) with humid CO2-air mixtures; fixed-bed tests with controlled inlet conditions. Feasible over 1–2 years for materials-focused study; implementation pathway to subscale prototype in 2–3 years.

**Novelty/Tractability/Relevance Scores:**
- **Novelty: 7/10** — Amine-loaded sorbents and hybrid materials are studied; integrated single-bed operation for spacecraft is less common. Moderate novelty in materials and systems integration.
- **Tractability: 7/10** — Bench experiment and modeling tractable; prototype qualification would require more effort.
- **Relevance: 7/10** — Applicability to modular future habitats; potential mass savings 15–25% if selectivity maintained. Not as critical as liquid amine separation but valuable for distributed architectures.

**Score: 7/10** — Medium-high-priority systems engineering gap with materials innovation angle.

### Gap 4: Trace Contaminant Control Adaptation to Partial-Gravity Off-Gassing Profiles
**Prior Work:** ISS Trace Contaminant Control Subsystem (TCCS) empirically developed for microgravity crew (6–7 persons, ~0.7 kg/day metabolic CO2). Crew off-gassing composition (alcohols, aldehydes, ammonia, volatile hydrocarbons) measured on ISS. Lunar and Mars hab crews will experience partial gravity (0.16g, 0.38g) affecting metabolism, thermal cycling of equipment (Lunar day/night ~14 days), and outgassing kinetics.

**Why It Matters:** TCCS contaminant removal rates (GAC bed saturation, HTCO conversion efficiency) calibrated to ISS microgravity off-gassing. Partial-gravity crews may exhibit different respiration/perspiration profiles and off-gassing temperatures (thermal cycling affects volatilization rates). HERA analog data does not capture gravity effects. Oversizing TCCS for unknown partial-gravity margins adds mass/power; undersizing risks SMAC violations.

**Tractability:** HERA habitat modification to test contaminant generation and TCCS performance under partial-gravity simulation (e.g., lower-body negative pressure, rotating-platform analog, treadmill loading). Metabolic off-gassing chamber study with partial-gravity analog over 6–12 months. Modeling of thermal cycling effects on equipment off-gassing using established chemical kinetics.

**Novelty/Tractability/Relevance Scores:**
- **Novelty: 6/10** — Off-gassing science is established; application to partial-gravity is application-specific, not fundamental novelty.
- **Tractability: 6/10** — Requires HERA facility time or analog rig; achievable but not trivial. Modeling + targeted analog testing feasible in 2–3 years.
- **Relevance: 6/10** — Direct support for Lunar Gateway/surface hab design; impact depends on crew size and mission duration. Not as critical as CO2 removal but important for safety margins.

**Score: 6/10** — Medium-priority application-driven gap.

### Gap 5: Oxygen Generation Efficiency and Water Management Under Partial-Gravity Electrolysis
**Prior Work:** ISS OGA solid polymer electrolyte (SPE) design mature and flight-proven; >16,000 lbm O₂ produced. Literature notes gas-bubble behavior and electrode wetting differ in microgravity. No flight data on OGA performance in partial gravity (Moon, Mars).

**Why It Matters:** Lunar and Mars habitats require OGA for O₂ generation; electrode flooding and bubble removal efficiency differ under 0.16g and 0.38g vs. microgravity and 1g. Water availability is ISRU opportunity (lunar ice, Martian atmosphere/subsurface); OGA water consumption and regeneration efficiency may be improved by understanding partial-gravity bubble dynamics. Current SPE cells designed for microgravity; 1g terrestrial operation not identical.

**Tractability:** Partial-gravity electrolysis cell testing in rotating-chamber rig or drop-tower parabolic-flight campaigns. Electrode materials and flow-field geometry parametric study feasible. Bench-scale SPE stack test with controlled water inlet, pressure, and partial-gravity simulation (centrifuge).

**Novelty/Tractability/Relevance Scores:**
- **Novelty: 6/10** — SPE electrolysis is established; partial-gravity optimization is novel but engineering-focused, not fundamental science.
- **Tractability: 5/10** — Requires partial-gravity facility access (parabolic flights, centrifuge); more complex than ground testing. 3–4 year development cycle realistic.
- **Relevance: 7/10** — Critical for Moon/Mars missions; water-limited environments increase OGA importance. Direct mission enabler.

**Score: 6/10** — Medium-high-priority, tied to resource constraints on destination.

### Gap 6: Sabatier and Bosch Reactor Thermal Integration and Control Under Transient Crew Load
**Prior Work:** ISS Sabatier reactor flight-proven; Bosch process extensively studied but not flight-qualified. Both reactors are exothermic (ΔH = −206 kJ/mol Sabatier, −131 kJ/mol Bosch); thermal management via radiator panels and heat-pipe integration. Current designs assume steady-state or slow crew load variation.

**Why It Matters:** Mars transit or long-duration Moon missions involve variable crew activity (exercise, sleep, EVA prep) causing transient CO2 production (factor ~2–3 variation). Sabatier/Bosch reactor inlet CO2 and H₂ flow rates fluctuate; exothermic heat release and catalyst bed temperature oscillations may reduce conversion efficiency or require rapid coolant modulation. Bosch reactor is more sensitive (solid carbon formation is temperature-critical). Control strategies for variable load are not well-published.

**Tractability:** Bench-scale Sabatier or Bosch reactor test stand with variable inlet gas flow and temperature control. Modeling of transient thermal and reaction kinetics. 12–24 month experimental + analysis feasible.

**Novelty/Tractability/Relevance Scores:**
- **Novelty: 6/10** — Reactor dynamics are understood; transient operation under duty-cycle variation is application-specific gap.
- **Tractability: 7/10** — Bench testing and modeling straightforward; no microgravity or exotic hardware required.
- **Relevance: 6/10** — Relevant to Bosch implementation and Sabatier reliability on long missions; impacts system robustness and automaton complexity.

**Score: 6/10** — Medium-priority control/integration gap.
